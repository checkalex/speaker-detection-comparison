01

Identical audio, isolated detection

Both models received the same 16 kHz mono WAV for each clip. This compares Precision-3 with Nemotron 3 Diarization, not the full Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe pipeline.

02

Model-assisted labels, subtitle-based times

The first four references began with Checksub’s original subtitle exports and speaker assignments, which may favor the Checksub/Precision-3 baseline. Friends Christmas began with NVIDIA speaker labels, which may favor NVIDIA’s measured agreement. All five use Checksub transcription/alignment cue times; neither diarization model independently supplied those timings. Human review does not make this a blind reference. Only Dave’s chorus has explicit overlap annotation.

03

Strict, consistent scoring

Zero boundary collar; overlap included; full-clip evaluation. Speaker IDs are matched to names globally and one-to-one, so splitting or merging identities is penalized.

04

A small, specialized sample

Three animation clips and two Friends compilations, all in English, with music, laughter and short turns. Dave and Friends Christmas exceed NVIDIA’s eight-speaker capacity. Customer recordings and other languages need separate evaluation.

05

Speed and cost need a separate test

Precision-3 used a hosted API; NVIDIA used local GPU inference. Queueing, upload and model load have different scopes. This report makes no apples-to-apples speed or cost claim.

05CONCLUSION

Keep Precision-3
as the baseline, for now.

For assigning subtitles to the correct person, keep Precision-3 as the provisional default. It has less wrong-speaker time in all five clips. NVIDIA leads total DER, largely through fewer detections outside the subtitle reference.

NVIDIA remains a promising local candidate. The four original references may favor the Checksub baseline; Christmas may favor NVIDIA. Review speech boundaries and identities independently before a replacement decision. Neither system recovers all five annotated chorus voices.

PROVISIONAL RECOMMENDATION

BEFORE A REPLACEMENT DECISION

1

Tighten the reference

Annotate actual speech boundaries, identities and overlaps independently of subtitle display timing and either model’s predictions. Check the intervals currently counted as extra speech.

2

Broaden and match the test set

Add customer-style interviews in our working languages; compare recordings within the eight-speaker limit, with larger casts assessed separately.

3

Measure the complete trade-off

Compare correction time, deployment cost and latency under consistent conditions, then inspect the final Checksub editor output.
